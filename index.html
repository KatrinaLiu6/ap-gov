01 / THE PROPOSAL

A short-term bridge through a financial emergency.

A proposal to help low-income renters avoid homelessness after a temporary financial emergency by providing up to three months of emergency rent assistance.

Up to 3 monthsRent assistance, including overdue rent, capped at $6,000 per household in 36 months.
$250 million per yearA proposed three-year federal pilot. At least 90% of the funding goes to rent assistance.
Local delivery, federal oversightHUD distributes funds through the existing Emergency Solutions Grants network.

2

3

Pay

Evaluate

HUD tracks spending and housing stability after assistance.

The dollar amounts, eligibility limits, and program duration are proposed policy choices, not research estimates.

02 / THE EVIDENCE

Why prevention deserves a closer look.

The local problem

San Diego County's 2026 Point-in-Time count recorded 9,803 people experiencing homelessness. This is a snapshot of one night, not the total number who experienced homelessness during the year.

San Diego County 2026 homelessness count ↗

Pressure on renters

In 2023, 49.7 percent of U.S. renter households for which rent burden was calculated spent more than 30 percent of income on housing. This statistic describes housing costs; it does not mean that all these households face eviction.

Census Bureau data on renters' housing costs ↗

Evidence for prevention

A randomized study in Santa Clara County found that people offered emergency financial assistance were 81 percent less likely to become homeless within six months than the comparison group. This is a relative reduction in that study, not a guaranteed result for this proposed national pilot.

Notre Dame research on emergency assistance ↗

What already exists

HUD's Emergency Solutions Grants already support homelessness prevention and rental assistance. This proposal adds a dedicated three-year funding stream with its own eligibility and benefit limits, using that existing delivery network.

03 / PROPOSED LEGISLATION

Housing Stability Act

Representative Katrina Liu · Domestic Affairs Committee
H.R. number to be assigned in class

A Bill to establish supplemental emergency rental assistance to prevent homelessness.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled, that

Purpose and Goals

A temporary loss of income or an unexpected expense can leave low-income renters unable to pay rent. This Act aims to prevent homelessness by helping eligible households resolve a short-term housing emergency, while limiting spending and requiring public reporting of results.

Section 1 Establishment of the program

This Act may be cited as the Housing Stability Act. The Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) shall establish a three-year emergency rental assistance pilot using the existing Emergency Solutions Grants network. State and local grant recipients may work with nonprofit housing organizations to administer assistance.

The eligibility, benefit, and spending rules in this Act apply only to these supplemental funds and do not reduce assistance available through existing programs. Other applicable housing safety and civil rights requirements remain in effect.

Section 2 Eligibility

A household must rent its primary home, have income at or below 50 percent of the area median income adjusted for household size, and lack sufficient available resources to pay overdue or immediately due rent. The household must document a financial hardship within the previous six months, such as job loss, reduced work hours, or an unexpected medical expense, and a risk of losing its housing within 30 days because of unpaid rent.

Recipients shall prioritize households at or below 30 percent of area median income and those facing the earliest eviction deadlines. Assistance is subject to available funds.

Section 3 Assistance and safeguards

An eligible household may receive payment for up to three months of rent, including overdue rent, with a total limit of $6,000 during any 36-month period. Payments shall go directly to the landlord after verification of the rental obligation. The landlord must agree in writing to credit the payment to the tenant and not evict the tenant for nonpayment of the rent covered by that payment.

The same rent expense may not be paid twice by public assistance programs. Applications shall be available online and in person. Recipients shall decide complete applications within 10 business days, give written reasons for denials, and provide a review process. Applicants facing an eviction deadline within seven days shall receive expedited review. Recipients shall also offer referrals to housing counseling and legal aid.

Section 4 Funding

Congress hereby appropriates $250 million from general Treasury funds for each of the pilot's three fiscal years, for a total of $750 million. HUD shall distribute the funds through the Emergency Solutions Grants allocation formula. These funds shall supplement existing housing and shelter funding.

At least 90 percent of each year's appropriation shall be used for rental assistance. No more than 10 percent may be used for all federal and local administration, outreach, referrals, audits, and evaluation combined. Funds reserved for evaluation shall remain available until the final report is due.

Section 5 Implementation and accountability

HUD shall issue implementation rules within 180 days of enactment. The pilot shall begin with the first federal fiscal year starting at least 180 days after enactment and shall operate for three fiscal years.

HUD shall publish annual reports showing spending, households assisted, processing times, and housing stability six and twelve months after assistance. Reports must identify missing follow-up data and protect personal information. HUD shall audit a sample of recipients and recover misused funds through existing procedures. A final evaluation shall be submitted to Congress within 18 months after the pilot ends. Continuing the program shall require new congressional approval.

Conclusion

The Housing Stability Act would give eligible renters temporary help before an unpaid rent bill leads to homelessness. Its assistance limits, direct payments, and reporting requirements would allow Congress to assess whether the program should continue.

04 / LETTER TO CONGRESS

Asking for federal action.

Draft letter to U.S. Representative Scott Peters of California.

November 4, 2026

Dear Representative Peters,

I am an AP Government student in San Diego asking you to support emergency rental assistance to prevent homelessness. The Regional Task Force on Homelessness counted 9,803 people experiencing homelessness in San Diego County in 2026. That one-night estimate shows why housing stability remains an important local issue.

For our class simulation, I propose the Housing Stability Act. It would provide eligible low-income renters up to three months of rent assistance, capped at $6,000, after a financial emergency. I believe temporary help can give people a chance to remain housed while they recover financially. Please support legislation that expands targeted homelessness prevention. Thank you for considering this proposal.

Sincerely,

06 / COMMITTEE DISCUSSION

Questions worth debating.

Why create this if assistance already exists?

The bill adds dedicated prevention funding through the existing ESG network. It changes the resources available and sets specific rules for those additional funds. It does not claim to invent rental assistance.

How would you pay for it?

The proposal appropriates $250 million a year from general Treasury funds for three years. That is new federal spending with a real budget tradeoff; the bill does not include a new tax or an offsetting cut.

How many households could it help?

For illustration, $225 million in yearly rental assistance divided by the maximum $6,000 award equals 37,500 full awards. This is a budget calculation, not a forecast. Actual enrollment would depend on spending and the size of each award.

Could someone receive money without paying rent?

The recipient must verify eligibility and the rental obligation. Payments go directly to landlords, duplicate payments for the same rent are barred, and HUD audits a sample of recipients.

What happens when three months of help ends?

Some renters will need longer-term assistance. This pilot addresses temporary emergencies and offers referrals. It does not replace affordable housing, shelter, or other services for people already experiencing homelessness.

What compromise could you accept?

One option is reducing the yearly budget to $150 million while preserving the three-year trial and evaluation. The tradeoff is that fewer households could receive assistance.
